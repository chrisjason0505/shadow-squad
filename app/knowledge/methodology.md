# How Shadow Squad picks a replacement

## The question the engine answers
For a club C, a season t and a departing starter S at fine position P (CB, FB, DM, CM, AM, W or ST), Shadow Squad ranks every player in the candidate pool at that position and asks: who would this club most plausibly sign to replace S? The pool is the season-t players at that position in the five leagues (Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1) who played at least 900 league minutes. A rank is a statement about plausibility and fit, not a guarantee of quality.

## Backtest mode versus live mode
Backtest mode ("2023-24 (backtest)" and other past seasons) recomputes the list from the Parquet lake using FBref per-90 statistics, and every model it uses was fitted only on seasons up to t, so the list is what the engine would have said that summer. The app then shows who the club really signed. Live mode ("Now") reads precomputed shortlists built from Understat and Transfermarkt data up to July 2026 with a separate live engine, because the FBref advanced statistics stop after 2024-25. Live shortlists exist only for the top clubs of each league (32 clubs); backtest mode covers every club in the five leagues.

## Style translation
A player's numbers depend on the team he plays in. Before comparing a candidate with the starter, his style-neutral per-90 numbers are translated into the buying club's context: stat_in_C = stat_neutral * exp(sum of significant beta[position, metric, term] * x_term(C, t)) * league_factor(league of C). The two style terms are x_dom (how dominant the club is: possession and territory) and x_press (how much it presses). The betas are elasticities fitted walk-forward, only on seasons up to the cut-off, and only statistically significant ones are kept. The multiplier is clipped to between 0.5 and 2. Validation on 1,319 real movers showed translation does not reduce prediction error versus raw numbers (mean scaled MAE 0.494 translated vs 0.495 raw; the 95% interval of the difference includes zero), so translated similarity is one signal among ten, not the whole story.

## The ten ranking features (backtest engine)
- sim_translated (translated similarity): cosine similarity between the candidate's profile translated into the buying club and the starter's actual profile at the club, both standardised as z-scores of log per-90 values within the position. 1 means the same shape of output, 0 unrelated, negative the opposite.
- traj_sim (trajectory similarity): mean cosine similarity of the candidate's last up to three seasons against the starter at the same ages when those seasons exist (age-aligned), otherwise against the starter's same seasons.
- pipeline_rank (club pipeline PageRank, log): PageRank of the candidate's current club in the transfer graph; high means the club regularly sells players up to bigger clubs.
- club_taste (ALS taste): an implicit-feedback ALS matrix factorisation score of how much the buying club has bought players of the candidate's archetype (a bisecting k-means cluster of playing styles, cut separately inside each fine position) in the past.
- hazard (move-up hazard): probability from a discrete-time survival model that the candidate moves up to a bigger club within the next 24 months. Captures the career stage at which players get bought.
- log_value: log10 of the Transfermarkt market value at season t.
- value_gap: absolute difference in log10 market value between the candidate and the starter. Small means priced like the player being replaced.
- age_num: age in season t.
- composite_perf_index: the mid-semester composite output index for the position.
- pattern_match: weighted count of closed frequent itemsets (trait patterns such as "high progressive passes + high interceptions") that past signings at this position shared and that the candidate also satisfies, weighted by log-lift.

## How the features are combined
A LambdaMART learning-to-rank model (LightGBM LGBMRanker) learns, from all signings by top clubs in earlier seasons, which mix of the ten features puts the real signing near the top. One query is one event; the label is 1 for the player actually signed. Ten models are trained on different subsamples and their scores are averaged (bagging). The rank shown is the rank of that average score. The interval next to it ("6 to 15") is the 10th to 90th percentile of the rank across the ten models: a narrow interval means the models agree, a wide one means the position on the list is fragile. For a season t the model trained on seasons before t is used where one exists; 2025 onwards use the model trained on all seasons; 2017-2019 fall back to the earliest model.

## Reading SHAP contributions
SHAP values (TreeExplainer, averaged over the ten bagged models) split one candidate's score into additive contributions, one per feature, relative to the average candidate. A positive value pushed him up the list, a negative value pulled him down. Contributions are in units of the ranker's score, which only has meaning relative to other candidates in the same query. Across 8,090 explained rows the largest average absolute contribution is value_gap (0.357), far ahead of log_value (0.053), age (0.049) and hazard (0.047); translated similarity averages 0.029. In plain words: the model first finds players priced like the starter, then sorts them by career stage, age and fit.

## Percentiles in the pool
The statistical breakdown shows, for each feature, the candidate's percentile among every candidate scored for this query (100 means the highest value in the pool). For value_gap and age a high percentile is not automatically good: the model learned that a small value gap and a younger age usually help.

## How good is the engine (backtest)
On 82 replacement events in unseen seasons 2020-2024 (average pool of about 99 players), the engine used by the app put the real signing in its top 10 in 30% of events (95% bootstrap interval 21% to 40%) and in its top 25 in 57%, with a median rank of 20. Random order gives 12% top-10 and median rank 43; picking the best young performer gives 27% top-10, median 29; picking the highest market value gives 37% top-10, median 15. Market value alone is a strong baseline: clubs mostly sign expensive players, and the engine's edge is in combining fit, price and career stage, not in beating the market at the very top of the list. Single seasons have few events, so treat any one list with humility.

## Live engine
The live engine uses only features available for every season up to now: sim_profile (cosine similarity of Understat per-90 z-scores: goals, non-penalty goals, xG, npxG, assists, xA, shots, key passes, xGChain, xGBuildup), traj_sim, seller_sales (how often the candidate's club sells to five-league clubs), log_value, value_gap, age, composite, min_share (share of league minutes played), and, kept after a forward feature-selection test, seller_bottom3, hist_club (the buying club has bought from his club before), hist_league (the club buys often from his league), same_league and min_trend. On 835 backtest queries it put the real signing in the top 10 in 30% of cases (random: 5%), median rank 28 out of roughly 200 candidates. Departure risk for starters comes from a gradient-boosted classifier (mean AUC 0.60 across seasons; base rate of departure 22%), so treat it as a weak signal. Contract dates are shown for context but are not a model input.

## Caveats a scout should keep in mind
The engine does not see injuries, wages, agents, release clauses, the player's willingness to move or the selling club's willingness to sell. It ranks against what clubs did, so it reproduces their habits, including the habit of buying expensive players. The backtest models were trained on signings by top clubs; for smaller clubs read the list as "players who fit the profile and price", not as a prediction of what that club will do. Market values are Transfermarkt estimates, not fees.

## Football glossary: what the per-90 numbers mean on the pitch
All profile numbers are per 90 minutes and are compared only with players in the same position (a winger with wingers, a centre-back with centre-backs) across the five leagues. "Top 10%" means the player is in the best tenth of his position for that action. Goal threat: goals, non-penalty xG (the quality of chances he gets on the end of, penalties removed), shots, and touches in the opposition box. Chance creation: assists, key passes (passes that lead directly to a shot), shot-creating actions (the two actions before a shot: passes, dribbles, fouls won), and passes into the box. Ball progression: progressive passes (passes that move the ball substantially towards goal, the line-breaking pass), progressive carries (running with the ball upfield), and dribbles completed (beating a man). Defensive work: tackles won, interceptions, clearances and blocks. In the air: aerial duels won. The live engine uses Understat numbers instead: goals, non-penalty goals, xG, npxG, assists, expected assists (xA, the quality of the chances he creates), shots, key passes, xGChain (his involvement in every attacking move that ends in a shot) and xGBuildup (the same but leaving out the shot and the key pass, so it captures build-up and link play).

## Club style and why numbers are adjusted
The engine measures each club-season on two style indices: dominance (share of the ball and territory) and pressing intensity. A possession-dominant side gives its players more of the ball, which inflates passing and carrying numbers and can deflate defensive ones; a deep-defending side does the opposite. Before comparing a candidate with the player he would replace, his numbers are adjusted to the buying club's style, so a centre-back from a side that defends deep is not unfairly marked down for low progressive passing, and a midfielder from a dominant side is not flattered.

## Why he is the best replacement: how to read the case
The list is not a ranking of who is best in absolute terms. It ranks who a club in this position, at this price level, would most plausibly sign to fill the role. A player tops the list when several things line up: he does the same jobs as the departing player (like-for-like profile), his career has followed a similar curve, he is in the same price bracket, he is at the age and career stage when players move up, his club is a known selling club, and the buying club has bought this type of player before. Price and career stage weigh most, then fit. A candidate can be a better footballer than No. 1 and still rank lower if he is far more expensive or plays a different role.

## Hindsight: where past picks are now
For past seasons the app shows where the players on the list went and what they are worth now. The current club is the latest the bundled data knows: the Transfermarkt dump if src/build_player_now.py has been run, otherwise the 2025-26 squads, a later recorded signing, or the last season in the feature lake (shown with its source). The value is the latest Transfermarkt valuation in the data (up to June 2026), and the change is against his value in the summer of the list. A player who "stepped up" later joined one of the top clubs of his league. Rising values and step-ups are a sign the list spotted the right players; a list whose top names stalled is a sign it missed. Market values are estimates, not fees.
